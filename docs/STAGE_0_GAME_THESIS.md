# STAGE 0 — THE GAME THESIS

### Working title: **SCARLANDS** (placeholder)

Status: **v1.0 — STAGE 0 COMPLETE. All decisions locked.** Repo: now authorized (see §19).

---

## 1. THE ONE SENTENCE

> **A survival-ARPG where you reshape the land itself with magic so that you can survive the night, while every scar you leave in the world attracts something that has adapted to it.**

That is the whole pitch. Everything below either serves that sentence or gets cut.

---

## 2. THE DESIGN PILLARS

Every future feature must serve one of these. If it serves none, it doesn't get built.

**P1 — Magic Is Terrain.** Spells don't just deal damage. They dig, raise, freeze, burn, and permanently mark the ground. Combat and construction use the same verbs.

**P2 — The Land Remembers.** Nothing you do to the world is cosmetic. Burnt ground stays burnt. Raised stone stays raised. The world keeps a record of how you've played.

**P3 — Every Light Is Yours.** The world is dark, desaturated, and hostile. Color comes almost exclusively from magic — yours and your enemies'. Light is a resource, a weapon, and a tell.

**P4 — Earned, Not Given.** Power comes from crafting, professions, and rare finds — not from a kill and a lucky roll. A mythic item should be a story, not a drop rate.

**P5 — Distance Is a Real Cost.** Travel is a decision. Cold, exhaustion, mana-dead zones, and terrain make "walking over there" something you plan for, not something you do idly.

---

## 3. THE SIGNATURE MECHANIC — THE SCAR SYSTEM

*(This is Stage 5, but it belongs in the thesis because the whole game hangs off it.)*

You gave me the lead here, so here it is:

**Every spell permanently scars the terrain, and the world's enemies adapt to your scars.**

- Fight with fire → scorched, glassed ground → over time, **ember-adapted** enemies migrate in: fire-immune, faster, they *want* the burnt ground.
- Fight with frost → rimed, brittle terrain → **hoar-kin** move in: slow, armored, they turn your ice into cover.
- Fight with lightning → fulgurite veins in the stone → **conductors** arrive: they chain damage between each other through your own scars.
- Dig, tunnel, and terrace → **things come up out of the holes you made.**

This gives us, for free:

- **Stage 6 (enemy ecology)** — enemy variety is generated by player behavior, not by a spreadsheet of HP tiers.
- **Stage 7 (systems interlock)** — see below.
- A reason to *diversify* your build instead of spamming one spell.
- A reason your base is dangerous: your base is the biggest scar on the map.
- Emergent stories: "the crater I made in week one is now a monster nest."

**Counter-play:** professions can *heal* land. A Geomancer/Warden profession lets you scrub scars, plant, and re-stabilize terrain — which is how farming, camping, and living off the land become mechanically necessary rather than decorative.

**Exit criteria for this mechanic (Stage 5 gate):** players change which spell they cast *because of what it will do to the ground*, not just because of damage.

## 5. RESOLVED & REMAINING

### 5.1 Platform — **LOCKED: browser now, port later**

three.js in the browser, architected for a later Godot/Unity port. This is the right call, but it comes with three non-negotiable commitments:

- **Simulation and rendering stay separate.** Game state (terrain grid, entities, inventory, scars) lives in plain data structures that know nothing about three.js. The renderer reads that state. This is the single thing that makes a port possible instead of a rewrite.
- **Lighting is faked.** Many real dynamic lights will kill browser framerate, and "dark with lights" is our entire art direction. So: mostly-unlit flat-shaded low-poly, 2–4 real point lights maximum (player, and whatever's actively casting), emissive materials plus bloom doing the rest of the work. Flat-shaded low-poly is genuinely cheap and hides an enormous amount.
- **Budgets from day one.** Chunked world with load/unload, instanced meshes for terrain and foliage, a hard cap on simultaneous enemies (start at 30), hard build-volume limit. Write the budgets down in Stage 1 and treat them as failing tests when exceeded.

### 5.2 Multiplayer — **LOCKED: solo now, co-op later**

Solo at launch. Co-op stays possible only if we avoid these traps now:

- No global singletons named `player`. Entities have IDs; the local player is just the entity the camera follows.
- Input becomes **commands** (`CastSpell`, `PlaceBlock`, `MoveTo`), not direct state mutation. Commands are trivially networkable later; direct mutation is not.
- Terrain edits are **events** on a queue, not immediate writes. Same reason, plus it gives us undo and replay for free.
- One authoritative simulation tick at a fixed rate; rendering interpolates between ticks.

We do **not** build netcode, lobbies, or sync now. We just don't build walls in front of it.

### 5.3 Death — **LOCKED: drop loot, go recover it**

Minecraft-style corpse run. Details to settle in Stage 3, with my defaults:

- Bag contents drop; **equipped gear stays on you.** Losing your build to one bad fight ends the session emotionally, not just mechanically.
- The drop persists indefinitely, marked on the map, but only one death cache at a time — die again and the first one is gone.
- Respawn at your last shelter or campfire. Shelter placement therefore becomes a real strategic decision, which is exactly the pressure that makes building matter.
- **Optional later:** the "permanent haunt" version, where the spot you died on becomes a scar that spawns something. Good hard-mode/NG+ feature, wrong thing to ship first.

### 5.4 Survival meters — **LOCKED: three, on two axes**

**Temperature** — a two-ended axis, not a bar that only empties. Hypothermia in the winter biome, heatstroke in the jungle. This is the single best decision in the design, because it means **no gear loadout is universally correct** and biome travel becomes preparation rather than walking. Fire magic warms you and cooks; frost magic cools you and preserves food. The same spells serve combat and survival.

**Water** — must be **purified**. Sources are everywhere; safe water is not. Boiling (fire), filtering (crafted), or freezing/thawing (frost) all work, at different costs.

**Food** — must be **cooked**. Raw food is a gamble. Cooked food gives buffs. Preserved food enables long expeditions.

**Design guardrail:** these three move *slowly*. They are expedition-planning pressure, not minute-to-minute chores. The failure state we're avoiding is the player stopping every ninety seconds to drink. Target: a full day of play on one well-prepared camp resupply.

**The campfire becomes the hub verb.** Cooking, purifying, warming, respawn point, light source in a dark world. That's four systems converging on one object — exactly the kind of density we want.

### 5.5 Persistence — **LOCKED: one persistent world**

Save-on-the-fly, chunked, returned to over months. Save architecture is therefore a **Stage 1** problem, not a Stage 12 one. Sessions target 30–90 minutes with natural stopping points (dawn, shelter completed, cache recovered).

### 5.6 Day/night and seasons

Implied by winter + camping + darkness, never stated. Night should be the primary pressure valve — the reason you build at all.

### 5.7 What the game explicitly ISN'T

Stage 0 requires this. My proposal:

- **Not** an open-ended creative sandbox. Building is survival infrastructure, not expression.
- **Not** a loot-shower ARPG. No screen-covering item explosions.
- **Not** a base-defense wave-shooter. Enemies pressure you, they don't queue up in lanes.
- **Not** a farming-life sim. Farming exists to sustain expeditions, not as the main verb.
- **Not** a story-driven narrative RPG. The myth is atmosphere and bosses, not cutscenes.
- **Not** multiplayer at launch.
- **Not** class-diverse at launch. Wizard only. On purpose.

## 6. THE CORE LOOP

**Descend / Explore → Fight (scarring the land) → Extract resources → Craft & Reshape terrain → Survive the night → the world adapts → repeat harder.**

The loop has a heartbeat: **day is expansion, night is consequence.**

## 7. THE VERTICAL PROBLEM (your hardest ask)

You asked how to get Minecraft-style vertical growth in a 3/4 view without it looking terrible. This is a real, solved-ish problem and the answers are specific:

**Terrace, don't tower.** Look at your own reference image — verticality reads beautifully there because it's expressed as *stacked plateaus and cliff shelves*, not vertical columns. Height comes in fixed steps (say 1.5–2m) on a grid. Nothing freeform.

**Cap the silhouette.** Hard build height limit (\~4 tiers). You go *down* and *outward* far more than up. Depth is where the danger lives anyway.

**Camera tilt around 50–55°.** Low enough to feel the height, high enough to read the ground plane. Slight, slow camera pull-back as you gain elevation.

**Occlusion dissolve.** Anything between camera and player fades to a translucent outline. Non-negotiable — this alone solves 70% of the ugliness.

**Floor slicing.** A slider (mouse wheel + modifier) that cuts away everything above a chosen tier when you're building or underground. Standard in colony sims for exactly this reason.

**Structural honesty.** Overhangs require support. This forces silhouettes that read as architecture rather than floating cubes.

**Reconciling with click-to-move:** Diablo-style click-to-move is genuinely bad for construction and terrain sculpting — the cursor needs to be free for targeting a tile. So: **WASD is the real control scheme**, click-to-move exists as an accessibility/comfort option and gets disabled in build mode.

## 8. TERRAIN MAGIC — THE ECONOMY

"Change the terrain with mana" breaks a game instantly unless it has a cost. The fix:

- **Combat mana** and **shaping mana** are the same pool. Reshaping the land leaves you defenseless. That's the tension.
- Shaped terrain is initially **unstable** — it decays back over \~1 in-game day unless *set* with a crafted reagent. So terraforming is free and temporary in a fight, expensive and permanent for a base.
- **Mana-dead zones** exist. In them you cannot shape, cannot blink, cannot heal. These are the "difficult areas to travel to" you asked for — the challenge isn't a stronger monster, it's the absence of your own toolkit.
- **Mana is harvested from the land**, and over-harvesting an area bleaches it (visually and mechanically). Another scar.

## 9. SPELL VOCABULARY — FIRST IDEAS

Two axes: **element** × **shape**. Every spell does damage *and* does something to the ground.

**Lightning**

- *Chain Lightning* — arcs between targets; leaves fulgurite veins that conduct future lightning (and enemy lightning).
- *Sunder Line* — a horizontal lance that carves a trench where it passes. Trench = cover = choke point.
- *Stormstep* — blink that leaves a static field at both endpoints.

**Frost**

- *Rimewall* — raises a wall of ice. Traversable. Melts. Shatters into shrapnel if struck.
- *Hoarfrost Bloom* — slows and freezes water into walkable surface. Your winter-biome traversal tool.
- *Glacier Drop* — vertical column, high mana, permanent terrain.

**Earth / Stone**

- *Terrace* — raises a shelf. **This is your core traversal + building verb.** It should be the second spell you ever learn.
- *Hollow* — carves downward. Mining, tunneling, trapping.
- *Stonebind* — sets unstable terrain into permanent terrain (the "commit" spell).

**Fire**

- *Emberveil* — a wall of flame that lights the area, consumes fuel, and burns vegetation away permanently.
- *Ashfall* — slow AoE that leaves fertile ash (farming synergy — burn to plant).

**Arcane / Void**

- *Gravewell* — pulls enemies and loose terrain into a point, then collapses it into a crater.
- *Prism* — splits one projectile into refracted beams off ice and glassed ground. Rewards terrain you already made.

**Design rule:** by Stage 2 we ship **four** of these, and they need to feel excellent. Not sixteen mediocre ones.

## 10. ART & COLOR DIRECTION

Your two references are in direct conflict, and resolving that is the point.

Take the **form language** from the low-poly reference — chunky faceted geometry, readable silhouettes, terraced cliffs, stylized foliage. Throw out the palette entirely.

**The world:** desaturated, cold, near-monochrome. Deep slate blues, bone whites, wet browns, dead greens. Heavy ambient darkness. Low-poly *noir*.

**The magic:** the only saturated color in the frame. Emissive, blooming, casting real light.

- **Player magic — the cool spectrum:** cyan, violet, and white-hot cores. This is "yours."
- **Enemy magic — the rot spectrum:** crimson, bile green, ember orange. Complementary, so a screen with both reads instantly.
- **Loot rarity** uses a *third*, quieter register — gold and pale amber — so it never competes with combat readability.

The screenshot you sent of the WoW paladin sheet is the right reference for the **information architecture** — dense, readable, stat-forward, gear-slot silhouette. Not for the visual style.

## 11. THE BIBLICAL LAYER

Handled as **war-mythology**, the way *Darksiders* or *El Shaddai* handle it — mythic, respectful, not devotional and not satirical.

- **Giants (Nephilim) are the boss family.** Goliath is the mid-game wall — a giant is precisely the enemy that a 3/4 camera and a terraforming toolkit make interesting. You don't out-damage him. You reshape the arena.
- **Samson** = a strength/endurance ruin-dungeon, themed around pillars and structural collapse. Perfect fit for terrain mechanics.
- **David** = a ranged/precision theme; the sling as an artifact line.
- Divinity itself stays **off-screen**. No depicting God as an NPC or a boss. The myth is the *aftermath* of divine events, not the events themselves. This is both the tasteful choice and the better creative one — ruins are more interesting than exposition.

## 12. STAGE 7 PREVIEW — THE MACHINE (taking the lead as asked)

```
FIGHT → scars the land
SCARS → attract adapted enemies + expose ore/mana veins
MINING → resources + deeper, darker, more dangerous
RESOURCES → professions → gear, reagents, tools
REAGENTS → make terrain permanent
PERMANENT TERRAIN → shelter, farms, safe routes
FARMS → sustenance → longer expeditions
LONGER EXPEDITIONS → further, colder, worse
YOUR BASE → the largest scar on the map → the thing that gets attacked
DEFENDING IT → more fighting → more scars

```

It closes. That's the test of a real loop: the last arrow points back at the first.

The elegant cruelty is that **success generates the threat.** The better you play, the more the world reacts. That's what makes it replayable without content bloat, and it's the answer to "why would I walk over there" — because the place you already ruined is no longer safe.

## 15. ENGINE — "CAN I IMPLEMENT A GAME ENGINE INTO THIS?"

Three different things this could mean. The answers are very different.

### A. "Should I write my own engine?" — **No.**

This is the single most reliable way to spend two years and ship nothing. You'd be building renderers and asset pipelines instead of building the Scar System. The interesting part of this project is the design, not the plumbing.

### B. "Should I build an engine *layer* on top of three.js?" — **Yes, and we already committed to it.**

three.js is a renderer, not an engine. It gives you nothing for entities, ticks, saves, collision, or input. So we assemble the missing parts from existing libraries rather than writing them:

- **Simulation layer** — an ECS or a plain entity registry. Owns all game state. Knows nothing about rendering.
- **Physics** — **Rapier** (Rust compiled to WASM). Fast, well-tested, deterministic-friendly, which matters for the co-op door we left open.
- **Fixed tick loop** — simulation at a fixed rate, rendering interpolating between ticks.
- **Serialization** — save/load from day one, because the world is persistent.
- **Chunk streaming** — load/unload/teardown as you move.

That stack *is* the engine. It's also exactly the boundary that makes a later port a port instead of a rewrite.

### C. "Should I just use Godot instead?" — **A real fork, worth naming.**

Godot 4 can export to the web, so this remains a genuine option and it hands you physics, an editor, tooling, and native builds.

The catch is that it inverts the plan: it becomes **native-first with a web export**, not web-first with a native port. Web deployment also introduces payload and hosting constraints that matter far more than they do on Steam/native builds.

**Recommendation:** stay web-first with three.js + Rapier. But formally re-gate this decision at Stage 8 (vertical slice) — if the browser is fighting us by then, Godot is the escape hatch and the sim/render separation is what makes taking it cheap.

### The constraint that applies to *every* option

Browser builds have a hard memory ceiling, and long sessions in persistent worlds are precisely the pattern that can expose memory leaks or teardown failures.

We want 30–90 minute sessions in a persistent world. So **chunk teardown discipline is a Stage 1 requirement, not an optimization pass.** Unload chunks aggressively. Free the geometry. Measure heap over a scripted 60-minute run, not a 5-minute editor test.

## 16. PHYSICS — WHAT "AWESOME PHYSICS" ACTUALLY MEANS

Full rigid-body simulation on everything is the most expensive feature you can add to a browser game, and it would also work against the determinism we're preserving for co-op. But here's the useful truth: **players don't perceive "physics" as simulation fidelity. They perceive it as consequence.**

Three or four dramatic, authored physical events read as "incredible physics." A thousand simulated pebbles read as nothing.

So the budget is: **physics is opt-in per object, with a hard cap on active bodies (\~50).** Everything else is kinematic. And we spend that budget on the moments that sell it:

**Structural collapse.** We already have support rules for overhangs. Make them real. Undermine a cliff and it comes down — on you, or on the enemies beneath it. This is a *weapon*. **This is also the Samson dungeon**: pillars, load-bearing architecture, bringing a building down on a giant. The theming and the tech are the same feature.

**Terrain slumping.** Dug ground settles. Sand pours. Snow slides. Cheap to fake with a cellular rule on the terrain grid — no rigid bodies needed at all.

**Ice as a physical material.** Rimewall shatters into real fragments when struck. Frozen water bears weight until it doesn't.

**Ragdolls on death only.** Cheap, high-impact, self-limiting.

**Rolling mass.** Boulders, logs, avalanches. Environmental weapons on a timer.

Rule: **if a physics feature doesn't change a player decision, it's decoration and it gets cut.** That's pillar-driven, not taste.

## 17. "WHAT IF THE GAME CREATED THE GAME AS TIME GOES ON?"

This is the best idea you've had and the most dangerous one, so it needs splitting in two.

### The dangerous half: infinite space

An infinite procedural world directly contradicts two things you already locked:

- **Pillar P5 — "Distance Is a Real Cost."** Distance can't be meaningful if there's infinite identical distance in every direction.
- **Your own Stage 4 exit criteria** — *"exploration creates curiosity rather than empty travel."* Infinite worlds are empty by construction. That's not a skill issue, it's arithmetic: authored meaning doesn't scale with generated area.

It also collides with persistence. Infinite + deformable + saved means storing every scar forever. (Solvable — store only *diffs from the seed*, the way Minecraft does — but it's real work, and it's work spent on emptiness.)

### The good half: infinite *content*, bounded space

What you actually described — *"the game creates the game as time goes on"* — doesn't require infinite terrain. It requires a world that **keeps generating new situations in a fixed space.** And we already built the machine for that: **the Scar System is a content generator.**

The proposal:

- **A large, finite, handcrafted-ish world.** Big enough to feel unbounded early. Bounded by things that *mean* something — mana-dead wastes, impassable ranges, a frozen sea — not invisible walls.
- **The world densifies instead of extending.** Your scars, your gear level, and elapsed world-time drive procedural generation *inward*: rifts open in places you've burned, giants wake in ranges you've mined, ruins surface in ground you've hollowed, weather systems shift with the season.
- **Procedural incursions and dungeons** generate in response to what you've done and how you've fought. Infinite content, finite map, every piece of it attached to a place you have a memory of.
- **Seasons on a long cycle** rewrite biome behavior. The winter zone in deep winter is a different, worse place.

This is strictly better than infinite terrain for your stated goal — a hobby you keep returning to for years. Infinite terrain gives you more of the same forever. A densifying world gives you a place that changes because you were in it.

**And the version you'd regret:** an infinite map where the interesting things are all within the first 500 meters and everything past that is procedurally generated wallpaper.

**LOCKED: finite and densifying.**

## 19. STAGE 1 CHARTER — CORE LOOP PROTOTYPE

*Repo is now authorized. Ugly graphics are not just acceptable, they are required — no art work happens in this stage.*

### Goal

Answer exactly one question:

> **Is reshaping the ground during a fight fun?**

Not "is the combat fun." Not "is the building fun." The *intersection*. If the intersection isn't fun, this is a different game and we need to know inside a month.

### Deliverables

- One \~120×120 tile arena. Flat-shaded grey boxes. Terraced height grid, fixed 2m steps.
- Camera at \~52° tilt, with occlusion dissolve and floor-slice already working. **These are Stage 1 features, not polish** — without them we can't even evaluate the thing we're testing.
- WASD movement, free cursor targeting.
- **Three spells only:** *Chain Lightning* (damage), *Terrace* (raise), *Hollow* (dig).
- Shared mana pool for combat and shaping. This is the core tension; it must exist on day one.
- Unstable terrain that decays back after a set duration.
- One melee enemy type. Basic pathing.
- Visible, persistent scars where lightning strikes. Visual only — no adapted spawns yet.
- Health, mana, fail state, restart.
- **Architecture:** fixed tick loop, entity registry, command-based input, chunk teardown scaffold, serialization stub.

### The one technical thing that decides this stage

**Enemy navigation must invalidate and rebuild when terrain changes.**

If an enemy walks through a wall you just raised, or fails to route around a trench you just dug, then terraforming has no tactical meaning and the entire thesis is untested. Every other item on this list can be ugly and approximate. This one has to actually work.

### What we deliberately DON'T build

No survival meters. No inventory or bag. No gear, gear score, or loot. No XP or levels. No crafting or professions. No biomes. No day/night. No giants, bosses, or myth content. No procedural generation. No adapted enemy types. No farming, camping, or shelters. No map. No audio beyond placeholder hit sounds. No co-op scaffolding beyond the command pattern. No art of any kind.

Every one of those is in this document and every one of them is Stage 3 or later.

### Exit criteria

1. We voluntarily want to play it again.
2. **We catch ourselves raising or digging terrain for tactical reasons mid-fight**, not just casting damage and occasionally remembering we can shape the ground.

Criterion 2 is the real gate. Criterion 1 without criterion 2 means we built a mediocre ARPG.

### Kill criteria

If after roughly two weeks of iteration terraforming still feels like a chore interrupting combat, we **stop and revisit §12.** We do not add crafting, loot, or biomes hoping they'll make it fun. That is precisely the failure mode this whole process exists to prevent.
